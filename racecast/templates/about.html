What a rating is

A speed rating is a race, corrected for everything about it that was not the athlete, and expressed against the field the athlete competes in. 100 is the mean of that pool; higher is faster. Real values run roughly 30 to 170.

Because the scale is pool-relative, a rating is only meaningful within its pool. A 140 in high school boys and a 140 in middle school boys are not the same performance - each is measured against its own field.

What each rating means as a time, from the top of the boards to the bottom: rating to time.

How a result becomes a rating

  1. Distance. Every time is converted to a 5K-equivalent, using a curve fitted per pool rather than a fixed formula. This is because older runners are a lot better at longer distances than younger runners. However, this does lead to some less than ideal conversions for longer distances.
  2. Geometry. Track results are corrected for the track itself: length, banking, indoor or outdoor. These corrections do not show in course difficulty, although they may show a residual affect.
  3. Era. Times drift across decades due to shoes, surfaces, and training. The era correction is what lets a 2010 season sit beside a 2026 one.
  4. Weather. Heat and wind are corrected where the conditions are known.
  5. The course. Every venue gets a difficulty solved from athletes who raced both there and elsewhere, the whole corpus at once, not course by course. This is what makes two races on different courses comparable at all. Courses with not enough links are forced to a rating of 0.0. The average XC rating is about +0.01, and for TF about -0.015.

Where it is still wrong

One race is not a season

The same athlete, at the same venue, in the same season, varies by about 3.3% race to race - roughly 4 rating points. That is not measurement error; it is what racing is like. It is why the default board ranks athletes by their season average and the single-race board is labelled performances rather than athletes.

A great race by an ordinary athlete will outrank an ordinary race by a great one. On a performance board that is correct. As a ranking of athletes it would be wrong, which is why they are two different boards.

Thin courses get a default

A venue that hosted one race, with athletes who race nowhere else, does not contain enough information to solve a difficulty for. Those get the sport's average rather than a confidently wrong number.

Some athletes are in the wrong pool

Level is inferred from grade, school and the races an athlete actually ran. Where those disagree - a ninth grader at a K-12 school, an eighth grader running varsity, a high school senior on a college start list - the inference can land on the wrong side. Since the scale is pool relative, a wrong pool means a number on the wrong scale. If you spot one, tell us.

Glossary

Rating
A race corrected for course, distance, weather and era, expressed against the athlete's pool. 100 is the pool's average runner; 133 means 33% faster than that runner. Roughly, a high-school boy rated 146 runs a 15:05 5K.
Rating to time
Every rating on the boards as a 1600, a 3200 and a cross country 5K (and the college 6K and 8K), pool by pool, in one printable table. The circled i beside a rating opens it.
Athletes board (season average)
One row per athlete-season, ranked on the average of their rated races. The default, because one race is noisy by about four points.
Performances board (single races)
One row per race. A great day by an ordinary athlete outranks an ordinary day by a great one, which is right for a list of races and wrong for a list of athletes.
Best times/marks
The clock and the tape, uncorrected: fastest time at one distance, longest or highest mark in a field event.
HS scale / own pool
Every pool (high-school boys, college women, middle-school boys…) has its own 100. The HS scale re-expresses every rating as if the athlete were a high schooler of the same sex, so a college and a middle-school number can sit on one list. Own pool shows the number as fitted.
Difficulty
How much slower times run at a course than at an average course, one scale for cross country and track: +4% is about 40 seconds on a 16-minute 5K. Ratings already account for it.
PR, SR, CR, CSR, R, RSR
Personal record, season record, course record, course season record, rating record, rating season record. A star means it still stands.
League, area, section, division, class, conference, region
The units a school competes in, inferred from the championship meets it attends. High school runs league → area → section → state; college runs conference → region → division.
Compiled race
Every division of a meet at one distance and sex, merged and re-ordered by time, as if they had started together. A race that did not happen, and labelled as one.
Ranked line
Where the athlete's latest season stands nationally, in each unit, in the state and on their team, at three or more races for seasons up to 2026 track; seasons from 2026 cross country on have no floor.
