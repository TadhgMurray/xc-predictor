# PART 2 — WHAT SHIPPED, AND WHY

## 2.1 The calibration gate — passed

cover68 0.664–0.695 (target 0.683), cover90 0.883–0.901 (0.900), err/sig
0.796–0.842 (√(2/π) = 0.798), **spread 0.97–0.99**. Full table in
`PLAN-2026-09-16.md` §7.

★ **`spread` is the headline, not coverage.** The failure that mattered was the
model learning to predict the pool mean far out — excellent loss, perfect
coverage, ranks nobody. It is not doing that, so the 44-week horizon twins
bought what they were built to buy.

Two bugs in the *diag*, not the model, produced the first all-zero table: the
target is raw **seconds** (`train.py` z-scores it at the loss with
`model.targetZ`), and it scored `range(50_000)` of 10,000,322 in athlete order.
Both fixed. The "sigma must grow with the horizon" check was also a false
alarm — sigma fell 0.0588 → 0.0501 from 20–44w to 44–104w while `|err|` fell
with it, because **a one-year gap is the same athlete at the same meet a season
later**, which is easier than six months out at another distance. The check is
now sigma against the error it actually makes.

## 2.2 Head-to-head odds and score variance

`predictTeam(sim=True)` merges `race_sim.simulate` onto the rows. The page gains
a **Win** column and a hover on each score: 10th–90th range in points, mean and
sd, win and top-three chances, how often the team cannot field five, and its
odds against every other team.

- ! **Never "0%" or "100%"** — 2,000 draws cannot tell 0 from 1-in-5,000. Reads
  `<1%` / `>99%`.
- ! **`team_rho = 0` is a floor, not a measurement.** A *uniform* race-day
  effect cannot change a single place (scoring is by place; a monotone
  transform of every time leaves the order alone — there is a test). What moves
  a team score is **within-team** correlation, which nobody has measured here.
  So the published `score_sd` is the narrowest the spread can honestly be.
  **Measuring it from historical races is a real open task.**
- Opt-in on the wire (`sim=1`); `draws` and `team_rho` clamped.

## 2.3 Best lineup — endpoint only

`/api/predict/lineup`, `predictTeamLineup`. **Not wired to a page.** Not the
fastest seven: five score and two displace, so the sixth runner's whole job is
to land between an opponent's fourth and fifth, which a ranking by predicted
time cannot see. Exhaustive under `ENUMERATE_MAX`, greedy with swaps above it.

## 2.4 Underrated recruits

`build_recruit_projection.py` → `recruit_projection`, pipeline step
`10f2_projection`. Sorts `proj` / `proj_gain` / `proj_rating`, and a Projected
column that appears only when one is chosen.

★ **A rating is `100 × pool_mean / adjusted_time` — a reciprocal — so the
model's predicted time over its own baseline IS the rating multiplier.** The
pool mean cancels; nothing is looked up. Ranked on the **residual** against
others starting at the same rating, because everybody at 80 is projected to gain
more than everybody at 115.

Falls back to the rating sort while the table is absent, so step 2 above is what
makes the column appear.

## 2.5 Logos

Three separate problems, three fixes.

**Black backgrounds.** Dropping an alpha channel: transparent is `(0,0,0,0)`,
and flattening that without a named background gives black. The keying code
always existed — the bug was *where it looked*. It sampled the four **canvas**
corners, but `normalise` centres every crest on a transparent square, so on any
non-square crest those corners are the transparent margin: it read alpha 0,
concluded "already has its own alpha", and left the black card alone. It now
samples the corners of the **opaque region**.

⚠ **Which upstream step drops the alpha is still unproven** — a JPEG re-encode,
a CDN resize, or a palette PNG losing its transparency index on load are all
candidates. And the `--worst` scan showed most grounds are opaque dark
*coloured* cards, i.e. genuinely the school's brand colour and not an alpha bug
at all. The keying removes both, which is why the symptom was worth fixing first.

! **A ground has to have something on it.** Moving the probe made a transparent
crest report its own mark's colour as a ground (the opaque region IS the mark,
and its corners agree) — and keying that empties the crest. `GROUND_MAX_SHARE`
refuses a colour accounting for essentially the whole opaque region.

**Random/wrong images.** The scraper does not invent a crest: candidates come
from `school_website`, it fetches that URL, and it takes whatever the page
*declares* as its mark. A wrong address-book row returns that domain's logo,
faithfully — hence a high school wearing the World Athletics mark.
`plausibleHost(school, url, kind)` (pure, no network) demands a real name token
of the school in the domain, or an educational/governmental host, or a plainly
district host, with a blocklist for governing bodies, aggregators and site
builders. Checked before the request and again on the page landed on.
Exemptions: anet (fetched by `team_id`, so tied to the right team by
construction) and owner overrides.

**anet now outranks the open web** (`KIND_RANK`). Resolution lost to provenance.
`athletics` still beats `direct`/`school`; an override still beats everything.
⚠ **This only decides who wins on a NEW fetch** — existing rows keep what they
hold, which is why step 4 needs `--redo`. (It said `--redo --replace`; see
PART 7 — `--replace` cancelled `--keep-better` without a word, and it also
overrode the guard that stops one (school, state) key losing a correct crest
to the other institution sharing it.)

**`--reground` prints progress.** It was silent through tens of thousands of PNG
decodes, which is indistinguishable from hung.

## 2.6 The horizon twin — fixed, waiting on re-extraction

⚠ **The measured failure:** sampling the whole validation split, realized
horizons topped out at **52.1 weeks** with **104w+ empty**, while horizon twins
were being produced in quantity. The twins existed; the long gaps did not.

The truncation was never the problem — `MIN_KEPT_RACES` is already 2 and the
window is sampled feasibly. **The draw was.**
`weeks = lo + random()**skew * (hi - lo)` with skew 1.0 is uniform over a window
whose upper bound is the gap back to the athlete's *second* race — which for the
one- and two-season athletes who dominate the corpus sits just under a year.

- `HORIZON_GAP_SKEW` 1.0 → **0.5** (favours the long end of each athlete's own
  window; same twin, different cut, no extra cost)
- **`_horizonLongTwin`**, drawing in [104, 208] weeks. Returns `None` for almost
  everyone by design, so rate 1.0 and all are kept.
- The extraction now **warns when zero two-year twins were produced** — their
  absence is invisible in the horizon count, which is how it got past last time.

**Takes effect only on re-extraction.** Fold it into the ISSUES G re-extraction,
then re-run `diag_calibration.py`. If the new 104w+/208w+ bands read LOW, drop
`PROJECT_WEEKS` in `build_recruit_projection.py` back to 52.

## 7.1 Why the name was never even contested

`authoritativeStates` learns a name's states two ways, and **both abstain on
exactly the schools it exists for**:

- anet's leg reads the states of the teams **the rows use**, through
  `results.team_id`. A tfrrs XC row carries no anet team id, so the University
  of Oregon contributed nothing and `oregon` came back `{IL}`.
- the college directory's `lookup()` answers `None` rather than guess when two
  states share a name — **which is the definition of a contested name.**

`{IL}` is one state, `len(v) >= 2` is false, so **`oregon` was not a contested
name at all** — and `si_team_state`, `si_dir_state` and `si_name_state` are
every one of them built for contested names *only*. Not one of them ran. The
clustering fell all the way back to athlete home states: one Oregon, IL, share
1.0000, with the university folded inside it.

★ That is also why the previous session's measurement read "Oregon ONE
cluster, IL, share 1.0000 — 36 home states, OR among them, all folded into

## 7.2 `school_team_link` — built 2026-09-16, read by nothing

## 7.3 Penn State had no logo, and neither could Williams

Two independent reasons, both now fixed.

**The crest queue dropped the college half of every collision.**
`anet_teams.teams()` INNER JOINed `school_identity` to the modal team from
`results.team_id` — so **a cluster whose rows carry no anet id was not in the
queue at all**, never asked about, never given a crest. That is the University
of Oregon, Williams College and Penn State's college cluster. `--missing` hid
it rather than showing it: *a pair that is not in the queue is not reported as
missing either.* It now falls back to the linked team, and **the link's own
state must equal the cluster's**, so the link for "Oregon" supplies
(Oregon, OR) and can never reach (Oregon, IL).

**`plausibleHost` judged an address anet had already resolved.**
`anet_teams.storeAddress` writes anet's `WebsiteSport` — the *athletics* site,
handed over per team id — into `school_website`. A college athletics site is
branded by its **mascot**:

    gopsusports.com  goducks.com   rolltide.com   guhoyas.com   gohuskies.com
    hawkeyesports.com   und.com    cuse.com       byucougars.com

No name token, no `.edu`, nothing "schooly" — **9 of 9 refused**, measured in
`tests/test_school_logo_host.py`, with Penn State's row reading
`address is not this school's (https://gopsusports.com/)`.

★ The check exists to catch a **guessed** address (Wikidata, a search). One
anet resolved by team id is not a guess — the same construction that already
exempts the anet mascot, and the same reasoning `fetchLogo` already uses one
level down ("an athletics site on a vendor host ... is linked FROM the
school's own page, which is the evidence"). So the **name** test is skipped for
`school_website.source` in `{anet, manual, override}`; the **blocklist** is
not, because `WebsiteSport` pointing at a vendor or a governing body is still
nobody's crest.

## 7.4 `--replace` cancelled `--keep-better`, silently

PART 1 of this document used to recommend
`--redo --replace --keep-better`. The whole guard block was inside
`if ... and not args.replace:`, so **`--keep-better` did nothing** and the run
did the opposite of what its flags said. "Right to wrong on a new scrape" is
what that looks like from the site.

- the two flags are now **refused together** rather than one being dropped;
- the **two-institution guard survives `--replace`**, because it is not a
  preference but arithmetic: where the level is *unknown* the crest key is
  `(school, state)` alone, one key holds one crest, and replacing a
  better-ranked one there is a loss rather than a swap. With a *known* level
  the two institutions are separate rows and anet wins as intended;
- the placeholder rule *is* a preference, and still yields to `--replace`.

```
 4,658 link;  7,146 stay their own schools
```

★ **The states were wrong until the run showed them.** `anet_team.state` is
OUR guess (the queue's pair, from where athletes race); `anet_state` is
anet's own. Every consumer read `COALESCE(state, anet_state)` — the guess
first. It printed Cornell NC, Ithaca WI, Tiffin IA, Hartnell TX, Cerritos
AZ, Iowa Central CC IN, Pima (AZ) CC as WA. Flipped in eight places
(`e8ddbb7`); all now correct, and Canadian teams resolve to QC/ON.

⚠ **Four wrong diagnoses before that, all from reading code instead of
plans:** a missing `team_id` index (built, and *neither plan uses it* —
~10% selectivity), a per-row walk of the 2,034-element array,
single-threading (`max_parallel_workers_per_gather` is 2 here, not 0), and
the table size (`results_tf` is 191M rows / 72 GB, not 54M). The actual
fault was the **`DISTINCT`**: the planner walked the whole table through
`idx_results_person` to get pre-sorted rows. `scripts/diag_link_plan.py`
prints both plans; 25× on `results`, 20× on `results_tf`.

! **`school_team_link` is keyed on the tfrrs STRING**, so a string genuinely
worn by two colleges gets one answer. `MIN_SHARE = 0.60` is the only guard.
Cornell (99%) and Monmouth (100%) came back clean, which says those strings
are not in fact shared — but that is the assumption to re-check if a college
ever lands in the wrong state again.

## 7.6 What is NOT fixed here

- ⚠ **None of this was run against the database.** The sandbox has no
  psycopg2, no Postgres and no corpus. Every claim above is from the code and
  from pure-function tests; the numbers that need a server — how many strings
  link, how many clusters split, whether Oregon (OR) now holds its own crest —
  have to come from the run in PART 1.
- **The reader still rarely passes `level`.** `school_logo.stampCrests` and
  `predict._stampCrests` call `crestUrl` without one, so a `(school, state)`
  pair holding two levels resolves through the level-less fallback. That is
  right while anet has not filed both, and it is why 7.4's guard matters. If a
  pair ever holds two *level-keyed* rows and no level-less one, the reader
  takes whichever comes first.
- **Three of `test_school_logos.py`'s seven failures are stale assertions, not
  bugs** — they text-match old spellings (`contextState(school, state)` now
  takes `trusted`; `race.html` passes its state as `sst`, which the regex does
  not recognise). The crest on a race page *does* get its context. Worth
  fixing so the suite means something, but nothing on the site is wrong.

# PART 8 — TWO SPELLINGS, ONE TEAM

`scripts/merge_school_names.py` → `school_name_alias`, applied by
`build_ranking_results`. Pipeline step **`09c_school_names`**, before
`10_rankings_prepare`, because the boards are what it fixes.

The case (owner, 2026-09-17) — one athlete, twice on one board:

```
15  Chiara Dailey  La Jolla (CA)  152.2  9:53.38  San Diego HS vs La Jolla HS
16  Chiara Dailey  La Jolla-CA    152.1  9:49.57  Brooks PR Invitation
```

⚠ **"Merge the substrings" is exactly the wrong rule, and the same message
proves it.** "Oregon" is a word prefix of "Oregon Episcopal", "Oregon Clay"
and "Oregon School for the Deaf" — four schools in three states. A
containment test on its own merges the set and undoes PART 7.

★ **So the name proposes, the athletes decide, and an anet team id vetoes.**
person_id is already merged across the feeds, so "the same people wear both
spellings" needs no spelling — the same join `link_tfrrs_to_anet` is built on.

| class | what it is | bar |
|---|---|---|
| `same` | the spellings differ only in decoration — a state code in any of the four shapes the feeds write (`(CA)`, `[CA]`, `-CA`, `, CA`), or a trailing `HS`/`College` | 3 shared athletes, ≥10% of the smaller roster |
| `prefix` | one key is a strict **word** prefix of the other — the Oregon Episcopal shape | **off unless `--prefix`**; 10 shared, ≥50% |

⚠ **And two spellings whose rows sit on DIFFERENT anet teams are two schools**,
however many athletes moved between them. That veto outranks any count.

! **Nothing rewrites `results` or `results_tf`.** The project's standing rule
(`app.py`: *"the raw tables keep the raw strings — the scrapers would fight
anything else; the qualified label lives in the derived layer"*). The fold
happens as the board streams, so the feeds keep their own spelling and **the
undo is a rerun with the table dropped**.

! **A bare `La Jolla CA` is deliberately NOT matched.** Without a delimiter
there is no way to separate a decoration from a name — "Washington", and
every school whose last word happens to be two letters.

! **The candidate search is linear, and its first version was not.** Bucketing
on the first word still leaves thousands of schools under "saint" or "north";
its own scale test took 90 s on 4,000 names. Both relations are defined on the
*key*, so `same` is a dict grouping and `prefix` is one lookup per word of each
name. 20,000 names sharing a first word now take 0.06 s, and nothing is capped.

⚠ **Unread against the corpus.** `--show 80` first. The bars above are a
judgement, not a measurement, and the dry run is what turns them into one.

# PART 5 — IF THE CRESTS VANISH AGAIN

`loadCrests` (`racecast/school_logo.py:144`) hides a crest **exactly four ways**:
`shared`, `override = 'none'`, `path IS NULL`, or the file not on disk. The
15-row sample that settled it on 2026-09-17:

```
/srv/venv/bin/python -c "
import sys,os; sys.path[:0]=['scripts','racecast','engine']
from database import getConn
from school_logo import pathFor
with getConn() as c, c.cursor() as cur:
    cur.execute('SELECT school,state,path,shared,override,sha FROM school_logo WHERE path IS NOT NULL LIMIT 15')
    for s,st,p,sh,ov,sha in cur.fetchall():
        f=pathFor(p)
        print(s[:24], st, 'shared=',sh, 'ovr=',ov, 'disk=', os.path.exists(f) if f else None, (sha or '')[:8])
"
```

**All four clean means the storage is fine and the running process is holding a
stale cache** — `loadCrests` fills once per process (`_CRESTS["loaded"]`), so
restart the service. That is what it was.
