**6. tfrrs and anet meets colliding on one meet_id** — proposal: split meet
pages by div_id (<100 tfrrs, ≥100 anet). Do not code the threshold from
memory; measure it first:

```sql
-- How real is the collision, and does the div_id threshold hold?
SELECT CASE WHEN r.div_id < 100 THEN 'lt100' ELSE 'ge100' END AS bucket,
       count(DISTINCT r.meet_id)                                AS meets,
       count(*)                                                 AS rows,
       count(*) FILTER (WHERE r.person_id IS NULL)              AS tfrrs_like
FROM   results r
GROUP  BY 1;

-- Meets that actually hold both populations (the pages to split):
SELECT r.meet_id,
       count(*) FILTER (WHERE r.div_id < 100)  AS low_div_rows,
       count(*) FILTER (WHERE r.div_id >= 100) AS high_div_rows
FROM   results r
GROUP  BY r.meet_id
HAVING count(*) FILTER (WHERE r.div_id < 100) > 0
   AND count(*) FILTER (WHERE r.div_id >= 100) > 0
ORDER  BY count(*) DESC LIMIT 25;
```

A better discriminator than div_id probably exists — tfrrs rows have
`person_id`-vs-`athlete_id` asymmetries (`panels.py ATHLETE_KEY` note) and
`meets_tfrrs` membership is definitive: `EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM meets_tfrrs t
WHERE t.meet_id = r.meet_id)`. If the second query returns real collisions,
split the page on the definitive source column, not a numeric guess. TF is
the open question the div_id guess can't answer — same census against
`results_tf` first.

## L. 2026-08-30, engine methodology review

**62. The Ultimook elite lift is REAL and is NOT the tilt.** The observation
that motivated tilt -- elites +10 on every other race they ran that season,
everyone else stable -- cannot be produced by tilt, and cannot be dismissed as
selection either.
  - NOT selection/winner's curse: that produces SCATTER. Several elites all
    +10 at one meet is a COMMON FACTOR at that meet; independent good days do
    not correlate.
  - NOT tilt: at Hydrangea `ability_slope` measures −1.13 at rating 80-90 and
    **+6.78** at 130-139. A linear h() on δ = 0.12 can only produce about ±1.8,
    symmetric. `tilt.py` says so itself -- tilt accounts for 1.8 of the 6.78.
★ THE TEST THAT SEPARATES IT, and it uses data already held: take the elites
  who travelled to Ultimook and read their ratings at the OTHER destination
  invitationals they travelled to that season. +10 only at Ultimook = the
  course. +10 at every travel meet = peaking. At Ultimook alone, "elite" and
  "travelled and tapered" are the same set of people and cannot be separated.

**63. The sport offset is not an open problem — it is half-finished work.**
`pair_sportoffset.py` already fits the per-athlete sport slope
`y = alpha[athlete,season] + beta[athlete,season]·s + delta[cell]`, and has
already measured it on the real corpus: **a full split predicts 5.56% better
held-out, TF alone by 10.01%.** Specialisation is real and large.
  What blocks it is stated in `pair_recenter.py`: splitting severs the only
  edges tying XC venues to TF venues, and the operator's eigenvalue along the
  "TF up / XC down" direction collapses with K --
  `K=∞ 90.81 · K=5 60.46 · K=1 26.75 · K=0 0.00`.
  At full split there is ZERO information about which sport is harder.
★ AND THE FIX IS ALSO ALREADY WRITTEN: constrain beta's mean rather than
  penalise it (`pair_recenter`'s reparameterisation), which keeps every
  prediction identical and moves the mean offset into the difficulties.
! WHAT IS ACTUALLY MISSING: choose K by held-out prediction -- the file says
  "not a matter of taste: whichever value predicts held-out rows best" -- and
  wire the split plus the centring into the live solve.

**64. "XC looks overrated" has a named cause, and it is an ASSUMPTION.** The
XC-vs-TF level is not measured. It is identified by centring beta -- i.e. by
assuming the average athlete has no sport preference. `pair_recenter` states
outright: *"Nothing in the data proves the average athlete is sport-neutral."*
⚠ AND THE CENTRING POPULATION IS BIASED. Only distance runners race both
  sports; a sprinter never runs cross country. So beta is centred on a
  distance-specialist population, not on a neutral athlete. If that group is
  relatively better at XC, the level is set so XC courses read as harder than
  they are and every XC rating comes out high -- which is the reported symptom.
★ CHEAP TEST: re-centre beta on different sub-populations and watch the XC-TF
  gap move. If it moves a lot, the assumption is carrying the answer.

**65. There is NO gender offset in the engine, so the female problem is not
one.** Searched: no `gender_offset` anywhere; gender exists only as a suffix
on the pool name, and `rating = 100 × pool_mean / ability` normalises each
pool against itself. A level bias between genders cannot survive that.
Two places gender CAN leak, one of them a real suspect:
  - the shared δ, fitted from both the boys' and girls' fields at one venue;
  - ⚠ **the tilt, which is a function of RATING — a pool-relative quantity.**
    A rating-140 girl and a rating-140 boy get the same h() while running at
    very different absolute paces. Whatever is physically behind the tilt
    (running nearer your limit on a hill, form degrading, walking the steep
    bits) depends on ABSOLUTE PACE, not on where you sit inside your own pool.

    gender, systematically.
★ THE FIX IS ONE AXIS: refit `ability_slope` on absolute pace instead of
  rating, and see whether one curve then serves both genders where two were
  needed.

**67. What does a rating MEAN across pools? Undecided, and it is a product
question before it is an engine one.** `rating = 100 × pool_mean / ability`
means "this far above my own pool's mean, multiplicatively". So a 160 in hs_f
and a 160 in hs_m are equally far above their own means -- and that is ALL the
current definition claims. It does not claim equal percentile, equal rarity,
or that they would finish together. Cross-pool comparability under this
definition requires the two pools to have the same coefficient of variation,
which nobody has checked and which is unlikely to hold.
★ THE THREE CANDIDATE DEFINITIONS, and the site has to pick one:
  1. **Ratio to pool mean** (today). Simple and stable; percentile drifts with
     distribution shape, so cross-pool reads are not what users assume.
  2. **Percentile / rarity within pool.** "The 100th best girl and the 100th
     best boy." Robust to shape, matches what people MEAN by "same level",
     but loses the interval scale (a 10-point gap stops being one thing).
  3. **Fraction of a reference standard** (age-grading / World Athletics
     tables). Comparable across sex by construction, and it is what athletics
     itself already uses.
⚠ EVERY CROSS-POOL FEATURE ALREADY DEPENDS ON THIS ANSWER -- hs-equivalent

  built on definition 1 without that having been a decision.
