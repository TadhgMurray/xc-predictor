**6. tfrrs and anet meets colliding on one meet_id** — proposal: split meet
pages by div_id (<100 tfrrs, ≥100 anet). Do not code the threshold from
memory; measure it first:

```sql
-- How real is the collision, and does the div_id threshold hold?
SELECT CASE WHEN r.div_id < 100 THEN 'lt100' ELSE 'ge100' END AS bucket,
       count(DISTINCT r.meet_id)                                AS meets,
       count(*)                                                 AS rows,
       count(*) FILTER (WHERE r.person_id IS NULL)              AS tfrrs_like
FROM   results r
GROUP  BY 1;

-- Meets that actually hold both populations (the pages to split):
SELECT r.meet_id,
       count(*) FILTER (WHERE r.div_id < 100)  AS low_div_rows,
       count(*) FILTER (WHERE r.div_id >= 100) AS high_div_rows
FROM   results r
GROUP  BY r.meet_id
HAVING count(*) FILTER (WHERE r.div_id < 100) > 0
   AND count(*) FILTER (WHERE r.div_id >= 100) > 0
ORDER  BY count(*) DESC LIMIT 25;
```

A better discriminator than div_id probably exists — tfrrs rows have
`person_id`-vs-`athlete_id` asymmetries (`panels.py ATHLETE_KEY` note) and
`meets_tfrrs` membership is definitive: `EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM meets_tfrrs t
WHERE t.meet_id = r.meet_id)`. If the second query returns real collisions,
split the page on the definitive source column, not a numeric guess. TF is
the open question the div_id guess can't answer — same census against
`results_tf` first.

## 156. The race-day effect was a free spread parameter; now tilted like the course (2026-09-04, FIXED IN CODE, UNRUN)

    date        rows   field  mean 5K-eq    u        net
    2014-09-20  5,020  102.4  22:02.8   -0.1914   +0.002
    2015-09-19  5,545  101.4  22:08.3   -0.2013   -0.008

Cell delta +0.193; EVERY day about -0.2; net about 0. The cell was not
the mean of its days. Mechanism: the row was a + h*delta + u. Inside a
race, delta and u were separated only by h, so the pair (delta = +c,
u = -c) cost c^2 under two priors that are negligible against 60,000
rows and bought an ability-shaped spread of (h - 1) * c per row -- a
free parameter for "this field spread wider than its abilities say".
Woodbridge's afternoon JV fields in the heat spread wide, the solve
priced it as +19% course / -20% day, and the elite rows (h 0.91) got
-7 to -9% applied: the deflation the owner saw on that page in BOTH
run8 and run9, and the reason the +20% column never matched the rating.
Not a sparse-graph problem: a degenerate direction the priors did not
close.

Fix: u is tilted like the course, row = a + h*(mu + delta + u). Inside a
race delta and u are then exactly collinear and the split is the
priors' alone: delta the shrunk mean of the cell's days, u each day's
deviation -- what the column claims to show. `joint_solve.rowPrediction`,
the operator's adjoint and diagonal, `joint_golive.buildLive`
(`eff = h*(delta + u)`), `explain_joint_row`. `tests/test_race_day_tilted.py`:
with the tilt on, the row-weighted mean of u inside every cell is under
0.02 and u tracks the true day effects (corr > 0.9). The near-null
direction was also what CG was crawling along (260-500 iterations per
outer); expect fewer.

What this does NOT decide: whether Great Park's other days are the same
race (the 2014-15 fields at 22:00 mean vs 2022-25 at 21:00 with lower

## 190. The empirical exchange rate, and the offsets calibrated from it (2026-09-05, IN CODE, UNRUN)

`scripts/diag_event_pairs.py`: what athletes who run X for one event
run for another in the same season, no model. hs_m: the 9:00-9:10
3200 runners' median 1600 is 4:15.7; the 4:10-4:15 milers' median
3200 is 9:14.5. Each bucketing regresses to the mean (a 9:0x best is
partly a good day), so the truth is between: a 9:01 sits with a
4:12-4:14. The model after run11b: 9:01 = 132.1, 4:13 = 128.3, i.e.
9:01 = 4:06 -- the top band's 3200 credit about 2.5% too big.

Why the rows cannot see it (189): the top band is populated by the
event people are good at, and the row-level fit weights every 1600 a
distance runner jogs in a dual meet; the relation people reason with
is season best against season best. So the fix, the owner's "do the
relation thing": every (pool, event, band) class with at least
`DIST_CAL_MIN_PAIRS` (150) athlete-seasons that ran both the event and
the pool's reference event gets a PRIOR MEAN, the median over them of
log(best at the event) - log(best at the reference) in normalized
time, banded by the solve's own rating of the athlete-season (the
average of everything they ran, so a miler and a distance runner of
one ability share a band). `Design.calibrateDist`, refreshed with the
bands every outer iteration. Two things it had to get right:

- A fixed prior sd is nothing against fifty thousand rows (the first
  cut, sd 0.005, moved the solved e by nothing). The calibrated class's
  penalty is `DIST_CAL_SHARE` (0.9) of its own row information, so the
  solved e is 90% the pairs' median and 10% the rows'.
- The bests are COUNT-MATCHED. The min of 6 draws sits lower than the
  min of 3, and the 1600 is raced about twice as often as the 3200, so
  best-vs-best hands the 3200 ~1.3% MORE credit -- the wrong way. Each
  side of a pair is the best over the same number of races, a random
  subset (seeded) of the side with more.

`--no-dist-cal` restores the rows-only fit. `run_joint` prints, under
each pool's offset row, a `pairs` row: the median and the pair count
per band; the npz carries `dist_cal_mean` / `dist_cal_n`. Expect at the
top band hs_m 3200 to come DOWN from run11b's value by ~0.02-0.03 in
log time (the 9:01 to about 129, the 4:13 unchanged: the 1600 is the
pin; the level is a separate question, 148 decision 1). The pairs
ignore the venue and the day, which is why the rows keep 10%.
`tests/test_joint_dist.py` (7 tests: the trap world, count-matching).

## 192. Altitude is a visitor effect: acclimatisation (2026-09-06, IN CODE, UNRUN)

  altitude all of it. The go-live prints the median share the fields
  kept. explain_joint_row prints venue km, the athlete's home km and
  the credited km per row.

## 227a. The race-day term: what it does and does not do (2026-09-06)

u is one number per race (cell, day): the field's shared residual
against its abilities and the course's mean. In the solve it keeps a
muddy state meet from making the course read permanently hard, and the
tilt (156) makes delta the cell's shrunk mean and u each day's
