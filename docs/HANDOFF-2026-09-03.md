## 1. THE HS-EQUIVALENT FACTOR (#139), THIRD CUT, AND WHAT IT GUARANTEES

**The bug.** The owner saw a middle-school board of both sports, sorted on
HS-equivalents, put a 142.2 XC season under a 138.2 track season, and the
same across gender on team rankings. The factor was per (pool, sport,
distance), so rows of one pool wore different multipliers and the HS view
reordered them.

**Three cuts in two days:**

| cut | factor | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (09-02) | C(hs)/C(pool), C sampled over both sports | MS boards read 220: constants are in per-pool units |
| 2 (09-03) | geomean over sports of bare C(hs)/C(pool) | ms_m 1.41, still wrong, same cause |
| 3 (09-03, live) | geomean over sports of C(hs)/C(pool) x F(REP_DIST[sp],pool)/F(REP_DIST[sp],hs) | ms_m about 0.8 |

The F term is a unit conversion: each pool normalises to its own reference
distance (ms about 3000 m, hs 5000, college_m 8000, college_f 6000), so the
pool constants are not on one scale and the bare ratio is meaningless. The
old per-sport factor evaluated at the sport's reference distance (5000 XC,
1600 TF) is the right per-sport number; the geometric mean of the two is
the one number per pool. `_poolConstant` is per sport only and its
docstring says never to merge sports. `scripts/diag_hs_factor.py` prints
every piece.

**Does one number per pool remove the sport offset and the gender case? Yes,
by construction:**

- **Sport.** `hsFactor(pool, sport, distance)` ignores sport and distance
  and returns the pool's one number. Inside a pool the HS view is
  `own x k`, a monotone rescale, so no board that mixes XC and TF rows of
  one pool can reorder. The rankings ORDER BY (`rankings._hsScaleCase`)
  still has a CASE arm per (pool, sport), but both arms of a pool carry the
  same value now. `stampBoardRows` takes the distance branch or the
  representative branch; both land on the same number.
- **Gender.** Pools are gendered (ms_m, ms_f, college_m, college_f), one
  factor each. A team board is one pool by construction (`teams.py`:
  no `both`, no `all`; "high school boys and college women" are refused in
  one field), so on a team board the factor is a constant and a finish
  order scored on places is invariant to it. Across genders the two
  factors legitimately differ: ms_m maps onto hs_m, ms_f onto hs_f. That
  is the intended conversion, not an offset. The one list that mixes
  genders is a school roster, and `sortByShown` orders it on the numbers
  shown.
- **What is not fixed by it.** The factor is one number per pool, so the
  ratings it multiplies still carry whatever level error the engine has
  between sports (the 10c gap in §0). The factor cannot reorder rows; it
  also cannot correct them.

Caveats: `_FACTOR_CACHE` is per process, so the site needs a restart to
pick up new constants; the constants sidecar refreshes at step 13b. The
sanity rail is 0.5-2.0; a factor outside it returns None and the row keeps
its own scale (never 1.0-with-a-shrug, see the docstring).

Tests: `tests/test_hs_factor_per_pool.py` (six: same factor every
sport/distance, hs pools 1.0, rail, two scales never merge, reference
distances converted, source pins on `_REP_DIST[sp]` and `np.log(ratios)`),
`tests/test_scale_sort.py`, `tests/test_predict_squad_cap.py`.
