## 2. THE 9:01 (#107): FOUND, AND IT IS TWO THINGS

`scripts/diag_trajectory.py` follows every athlete who ran a given XC course
into the following spring, conditioning on the fall side only so Arcadia's
qualifying standard selects nothing, printing censoring, running both
directions, and testing means against the cells. Four courses were run.

**The tie is wrong on the engine's own terms.** A 9:01 is a top-1.4% spring
for a 15:40 Mt. SAC runner; the median is 9:39. Both selection-biased
directions bracket the engine's equivalence outside them, by 5 to 8 points.

| course | focus-band residual (obs − cells) | points at 130 |
|---|---|---|
| Mt. SAC (CA) | −0.063 | −7.9 |
| Woodbridge (CA) | −0.068 | −8.5 |
| Woodward Park (CA) | −0.045 | −5.7 |
| Detweiller (IL) | −0.016 | −2.1 |

Arcadia is exonerated: athletes run 1.8% faster there than their own spring,
0.4% at other invitationals, and the cell credits it 1.2% more than the
average track. Right to 0.2%.

**Piece 1 — the track distance curve under-credits 3200s, ability-shaped.**
`diag_exponent_season_best` (first run today): fast HS boys scale at k=1.16
even on season bests, slow at 1.12; the saved hs_m|TF curve runs 1.14→1.09
across 1600→3200. A top-decile runner's 3200 therefore normalises ~3.5%
slower than his own 1600. This is most of the ability slope in every residual
table and about 4.5 points for a 133. **Fix: refit the track curve on
season-best pairs.** Not done.

**Piece 2 — a California XC-versus-track level, ~3–4%.** At NXN (one cell,
nine states) the track sides agree within ±1% and the common residual is the
curve effect. So the extra at California courses is on the California XC
side. `scripts/diag_xc_bridges.py` (aggregates only, nothing streamed) shows
why: California bridges out of state 2.5% in XC and 1.9% in track against
10–30% elsewhere, and 103 athlete-seasons link CA to IL in XC over 15 years.
At ridge 0 a state's XC-versus-track split is pinned by nothing inside the
state. **Ridge 0.5 is the fix**, through every dual-sport Californian, and
the three trajectory reruns after the pipeline are the confirmation:

```
/srv/venv/bin/python scripts/diag_trajectory.py --course 'Mt. San Antonio College' --label 'Mt. SAC' --dist 4828
/srv/venv/bin/python scripts/diag_trajectory.py --course 'Woodward Park%' --label Woodward --dist 5000
/srv/venv/bin/python scripts/diag_trajectory.py --course 'Detweiller%' --label Detweiller --dist 4828
```

California residuals moving toward Illinois's is the result to look for.

**What was ruled out today**, so nobody chases it again: the Mt. SAC cell,
Arcadia's cell, the pool mean (common-mode, cancels between two rows of one
athlete), the exponent-by-ability hypothesis as a national effect, the
3200→5000 extension slope (absorbed by the sport level), and "XC is thinner
than track nationally" (XC bridges MORE; California is the island).

Side finding: Mt. SAC's real course is stored under two labels, 4715 m (the
true 2.93 miles) and 4828 m, as two cells. 4828→4715 is a downward override.

## 3. THE DEFINITION QUESTION, DECIDED FOR THE SITE

Owner's definition: a rating is skill at that moment, not expected
performance across a year. The engine's XC-versus-track level is a
population-mean October→April change and therefore booked the average
fitness gain as "track is easier". Nothing in the data separates surface from
season except athletes observed in both sports close together in time; the
owner rejected that estimator as too narrow. The definition stands as the
owner's; the engine change that would honour it is open.

Presentation, shipped today:

- **Rating 133 = 33% faster than the average runner in the pool** (speed
  ratio, which is exactly what the engine computes). A circled "i" beside
  every rating label says so on hover.
- **Course difficulty is a percentage against a typical course of its
  sport** (`racecast/difficulty_view.py`), because the raw zero sat between
  XC and track and every XC course read ~2% harder than it is. Mt. SAC reads
  about +4%, Woodbridge 0%. Raw value in the title. Display only.
- **Boards order on the scale they show.** Rankings sends `scale=` and the
  ORDER BY carries the per-(pool, sport) factor; school tables re-rank in the
  browser when the toggle flips (`scale-view.js resortTables`).

The "i" was rebuilt three times today (bar → disclosure → inline hover).
The owner wants it beside the number, on hover, circled, unobtrusive. That is
what is there now; if it is still wrong the macro is `_explain.html` and the
style is `.info-i`.
