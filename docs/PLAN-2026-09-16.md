## 0. DECIDED 2026-09-16

**Nuke a race on a pool record — but not naively.** Owner: *"nuke anything,
except make it the world record for diff anchors bcs for example college anchors
at 8k. So make it not naive and then nuke on pool records."*

`normalized_time` is **not** a 5k for everybody. `targetFor(pool)` is the
anchor, and the anchors are measured, not chosen:

| pool | anchor |
|---|---|
| elem_* | 2414 |
| ms_* | 3200 |
| hs_* | 5000 |
| college_f | 6000 |
| **college_m** | **8000** |

So the record to beat is the world record **at that pool's anchor distance**,
times that pool's floor factor — never "the 5k WR" against a number that is an
8k equivalent. Getting that wrong would condemn every college men's race in the
corpus, because an 8k equivalent is always slower than a 5k record.

    floor(pool) = recordPace(targetFor(pool), sex) * targetFor(pool)/1000
                  * PACE_FLOOR_SLACK * poolFactor(pool)

⚠ This overrules ISSUES K's row-only rule, knowingly. K removed race-level
nuking on pool records because the rows that beat a young pool's record are, by
construction, the ones the POOLING got wrong — and deleting the race deletes the
evidence that would repool the athlete. The owner has priced that against the
boards and chosen the nuke. Two things make it survivable and both must ship
with it:

1. **The pooling fix lands first.** Every rule in §3 exists to stop the
   mis-pooling that produces these rows. Nuke before pooling is fixed and the
   nuke eats real races.
2. **The condemnation is a table, never a column.** `impossible_race` rebuilds
   `impossible_result` from scratch on every run and nothing else persists a
   verdict, so a nuke is always undoable by one rerun. Keep it that way.

**No anet team → pro, and an unlinked school NAME → pro.** Owner: *"for w/o team
id yes, but also nuke if it's not linked to an anet row (the actual school name
not the specific athlete or race so it's not too overbearing)."*

* an **anet** row with `team_id` 0 or NULL → pro (`no_team`, already shipped).
* a school **name** that no anet team is linked to → pro. Name-level, so one
  odd row cannot move an athlete.
* ⚠ **`link_tfrrs_to_anet` has to run first.** A tfrrs row has no anet team id
  by construction, so "unlinked name" without the link table means *every
  tfrrs-only college goes pro*. The link is what makes this rule safe, and its
  dry run has not been read yet.

### What "looks good" should mean — measure it, don't eyeball it
* `--queue-only` first: the queue size decides whether `--min-rows` is needed.
* The closing line's three hold-backs: `kept`, `placeholder`, `stateless`. A big
  `stateless` means pairs with no state to file a crest under, which is a
  clustering problem, not a crest problem.
* A coverage census before and after: how many `(school, state)` pairs have a
  servable crest, and what share of `ranking_results` rows they cover. "Some
  logos are wrong" is unfalsifiable; "crest coverage went from 38% to 61% of
  rows and 210 pairs hold two institutions" is not.
* Spot-check the known collisions by hand: Oregon, Williams, MIT, Tufts,
  Amherst — label, crest and link on a **race** page, a **school** page and a
  **board**, since those are three different resolvers.

### Sections / divisions redo
Owner: *"now they are generally right but some are fucked up."*
`anet_teams.py` already stores anet's own unit hierarchy per team and sport in
`anet_division` (`scripts/anet_units.py --report` learns what its ids mean from

## 2. Rescrape anet + tfrrs — ⚠ one change first

**BLOCKER: `meets_tf` has no venue-name column.** Its columns are `div_id,
meet_id, meet_name, event_short, event_id, distance_meters, gps_lat, gps_long,
state, is_indoor`. There is nowhere to put a facility name, which is why a TF
venue key ends up being an id. This is a column plus the loader that fills it,
and it must land **before** the rescrape or the rescrape happens twice.

While that column is being added, take the rest of what a TF meet knows and we
throw away: the facility/venue name, the meet's own URL (for auditing a bad
parse later), and whether the venue is banked — `is_indoor` alone cannot tell a
flat 300 m track from a banked 200 m one, and geometry reads both.

### tfrrs column shifts
The guard is a per-meet **shape assertion**, not a spot check: every parsed row
must have a place that is an integer or blank, a time that parses to seconds in
a plausible band, and a name that does not itself parse as a time. A meet
failing more than a small share of its rows is rejected whole and counted — one
census line per run naming the rejects. A shifted column fails all three tests
at once, which is why the assertion catches it and a spot check does not.

### Anything else
* **Idempotency**: the rescrape must be safe to re-run over rows it already has
  (upsert on the natural key), or a half-finished run leaves duplicates that
  look like real double-entries.
* **Capture the scrape date per row** so "when did this get worse" is answerable.

## 4. Engine — the four ideas

* **Remove track difficulty (at least for now).** Indoor comes out ~4% *easy*,
  which is backwards — an indoor 200 m with tight turns is harder, not easier,
  and the sign says the cell is absorbing something else (season timing, who
  races indoors). ISSUES A. Removing it is honest: no correction beats a
  correction with the wrong sign.
* **The XC race duplicated into a TF race (AMO).** Same day, same clock, either
  sport → one race. Match on `(person_id, date, time_seconds)` and collapse.
  ⚠ Decide which copy survives *by rule*, not by which was scraped first: keep
  the one whose sport matches the meet's own sport, and when that cannot be
  told, keep XC in the autumn and TF in the spring. Print the collapses.
* **5k normalization = track 5k.** This is the anchor question, and it wants
  ISSUES J done first: `hs_m|XC` currently runs at a local exponent of 1.12
  where the same athletes' `hs_m|TF` runs 1.075 and the world records imply
  1.06. Anchoring the XC 5k to the track 5k while the XC curve is bent puts the
  bend into every conversion instead of removing it. **Refit, then anchor.**
* **Then the ladder, not a guess**: `run_joint.py --holdout` before and after
  each of these, so "better" is a number. An engine change that improves the
  boards and worsens the holdout is a change to argue about, not to ship.

## 6. The three predictive features (2026-09-17)

**✅ 1. `scripts/diag_calibration.py` — does the model's sigma tell the truth,
and does it tell it AT THE HORIZON.** Coverage of the 68% and 90% bands over
the athlete-disjoint validation split, bucketed by `days_since_last_race`,
with the bands cut where the twins are generated (2-40 weeks forecast, 44-208
horizon) so the two questions are not averaged together. It reports two things:

* `cover68` / `cover90` — 0.683 and 0.900 are honest. Low means every
  probability built on that sigma is overconfident.
* `spread` = sd(predicted) / sd(actual) — **not calibration.** With a flat
  44-208 week draw and a Gaussian likelihood, the classic failure is learning
  to predict the pool mean far out: excellent loss, perfect coverage, and
  useless for ranking. 1.0 is honest, 0.3 is shrinkage.

It also flags a sigma that does not grow monotonically with the horizon — a
further band claiming more certainty is lying.

**✅ 2. `racecast/race_sim.py` — `simulate()`.** Draws every athlete's time
from their own log-normal (sigma recovered from the `lo`/`hi` the page already
carries, so no second model load) and scores the race `draws` times. Out of one
loop: `p_win`, `p_top3`, the score's mean/sd/p10/p50/p90, `p_incomplete`, the
full head-to-head matrix, and every runner's place distribution.

⚠ **One scorer, not two.** The rules (five scorers, seven displacers, the cap
at what a team ENTERED, unattached and incomplete teams lifted out) live in
`predict._score`. `scoreDraw` is a vectorised transcription and
`tests/test_race_sim.py` asserts the two agree on 25 random fields — change
`_score` and that test fails, which is the point.

⚠ **And I had the correlation argument backwards.** A uniform race-day effect
cannot change a single place: scoring is by place, and multiplying every time
by one factor is monotone. It matters for the times a page shows and not at all
for who wins — there is a test asserting exactly that. What does move a score
is correlation WITHIN a team, because it shifts five scorers as a block, and
that is `team_rho`.

**✅ 3. `bestLineup()`.** The k runners of a roster that maximise P(win) (or
minimise the mean score). Exhaustive under `ENUMERATE_MAX` — C(15,7) is 6,435,
enumerable; C(30,7) is 2.03 million, not — and greedy from the fastest seven
with single swaps above it. Reports which method it used, how many lineups it
considered, and the five runners-up.

★ **It is not "the fastest seven", which is why it searches.** Five score and
two displace, so the sixth and seventh runners' whole job is to push the other
team's scorers back a place — making the best sixth man the one most likely to
finish BETWEEN an opponent's fourth and fifth, not the fastest one left. And
because the objective is a probability, variance counts: a runner who is
sometimes excellent is worth more when you are behind and less when you are
ahead. Neither is visible in a ranking by expected time.

⚠ **Nothing here is wired to a page yet, deliberately.** Order: run (1), read
the bands, and only publish (2) and (3) if the bands that matter are honest. A
confident wrong probability is worse than no probability.

## §7 THE CALIBRATION RAN, AND IT PASSED (2026-09-17)

Over the whole validation split, 50,000 scored:

| band | n | cover68 | cover90 | sigma | \|err\| | err/sig | spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2w | 351 | 0.667 | 0.883 | 0.0404 | 0.0330 | 0.817 | 0.99 |
| 2–8w | 2,089 | 0.677 | 0.901 | 0.0417 | 0.0336 | 0.806 | 0.98 |
| 8–20w | 17,962 | 0.695 | 0.899 | 0.0466 | 0.0371 | 0.796 | 0.99 |
| 20–44w | 26,983 | 0.684 | 0.894 | 0.0588 | 0.0487 | 0.828 | 0.97 |
| 44–104w | 2,615 | 0.664 | 0.886 | 0.0501 | 0.0422 | 0.842 | 0.98 |

Targets: 0.683, 0.900, and 0.798 for err/sig (√(2/π)). **Every band honest.**

★ **`spread` 0.97–0.99 is the headline, not the coverage.** The failure that
would have mattered was the model learning to predict the pool mean far out —
excellent loss, perfect coverage, ranks nobody. It is not doing that. Which
means the 44-week horizon twins bought what they were built to buy, and the
owner's push back ("a model wrapper could be more accurate, and is the entire
reason we made it at 40 weeks") was right.

### Two bugs the first run exposed, both in the diag and not the model

The first table read `cover68` 0.000 in every band with `|err|` inf and
`spread` nan. A bad model gives bad numbers, not inf.

1. **The target is raw seconds.** `train.py` says so outright and z-scores them
   at the loss with `model.targetZ`. The diag was undoing a z-scoring that was
   never applied, so `exp(seconds × target_std)` overflowed on every row.
2. **It scored the first 0.5% of the split.** `range(50_000)` of 10,000,322, in
   athlete order — horizons topped out at 50.1w, so the bands that gate the
   projection were the ones with no data. Now contiguous runs at 200 offsets
   across the whole split, which keeps the one-chunk cache working.

### And the naive monotonicity check was a false alarm

It asserted sigma must grow with the horizon. Sigma FELL from 0.0588 (20–44w)
to 0.0501 (44–104w) and the warning called it a lie. It was not: `|err|` fell
with it and coverage held. **A one-year gap is the same athlete at the same
meet a season later**, which is genuinely easier than six months out at another
distance in another sport. The check is now sigma against the error it actually
makes (`err/sig` vs √(2/π)), which catches a real lie in any band order.

## §8 ⚠ THE CORPUS STOPS AT ONE YEAR — AND THIS IS THE NEXT REAL TASK

`HORIZON_GAP_MAX_WEEKS` says 208. Sampling the **whole** split, the horizons
top out at **52.1 weeks**, 44–104w holds 2,615 of 50,000, and 104w+ is
**empty**.

★ **Why: the twin generator truncates to the last race at least
`HORIZON_GAP_MIN_WEEKS` before the target.** An athlete who races continuously
always has a race at about a year, so the gap lands in 44–52w essentially
always. Reaching two years would need an athlete with a two-year hole in their
history. The 208-week ceiling is a filter, not a draw.

**So a projection past about a year is extrapolation off the end of the
measured range, and nothing should publish one.** The fix is an extraction
change, so it belongs with the re-extraction already pending for ISSUES G:
draw a target gap in [44, 208] and truncate to the last race at or before
*that*, rather than to the first race past the minimum. Then re-run the diag
and see whether the long bands hold up.

## §9 WHAT SHIPPED (2026-09-17)

**Head-to-head odds and score variance.** `predictTeam(sim=True)` merges
`race_sim.simulate` onto the rows; the page gains a **Win** column and a
hover on each score giving its 10th–90th range in points, the mean and sd, the
win and top-three chances, how often the team cannot field five, and its odds
against every other team. Opt-in on the wire (`sim=1`); `draws` and `team_rho`
are clamped.

! **Never "0%" or "100%".** 2,000 draws cannot tell 0 from 1-in-5,000. Reads
`<1%` / `>99%`.

! **`team_rho=0` is a floor, not a measurement.** Nobody has measured
within-team correlation here, so the published `score_sd` is the narrowest the
spread can honestly be. Measuring it is worth doing.

**Best lineup.** `/api/predict/lineup`, `predictTeamLineup`. Not wired to a
page yet — the endpoint works and returns decorated rows.

**Underrated recruits.** `build_recruit_projection.py` → `recruit_projection`,
pipeline step `10f2_projection`. Three sorts (`proj`, `proj_gain`,
`proj_rating`) and a Projected column that appears only when one is chosen.

★ **The rating is a reciprocal of time, so the model's predicted time over its
own baseline IS the rating multiplier** — the pool mean cancels and nothing has
to be looked up. Ranked on the residual against others starting at the same
rating, because everybody at 80 is projected to gain more than everybody at 115.

**Black logo grounds.** `_flatGround` probed the CANVAS corners, which on any
non-square crest are the transparent margin `normalise` added — so it concluded
"already has its own alpha" and left the dark card alone. It now probes the
corners of the opaque region. Repair needs no rescrape, because the ground is
in the stored PNG:

    python scripts/scrape_school_logos.py --reground --dry-run
    python scripts/scrape_school_logos.py --reground --write
