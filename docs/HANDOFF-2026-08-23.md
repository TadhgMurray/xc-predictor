## 2. Thread: the override rebuild

### 2.1 What was wrong and is fixed

- **All three passes scaled from the scraped distance**, not the one the
  ratings were built at. `dist_override` is first in the precedence chain
  everywhere else. Lehigh 44518/191150: override 6000, `meets` 8000, so every
  implied distance came out 1.33× too long, got written, and the next run
  repeated it from the new wrong base.
- **`--as-if-wiped` moves what the ratings were built at, so it moves the
  label too.** Swedetown 199300/798809 proposed 2400→1200 with the override as
  label, against 5000→2500 with it reverted.
- **The label rule lived in three copies** and only pass 1 ever got fixed.
  Now one `_LABEL_LATERAL` spliced into all three.
- **The site never read `dist_override`** — Ox Bow rated as 5000 and displayed
  as 8046.
- **The corpus ladder was a comb.** De-combed by magnitude share; 94 rungs → 58.

### 2.2 The gap table now judges every finisher

`--gap-from times` (the default) computes the rating itself for every row:

```
100 · pool_mean · exp(delta_cell) / normalized_time
```

Why: gating on `speed_rating IS NOT NULL` made the detector weakest exactly
where the fault is largest — a wrong distance makes times look impossible, the
pace guard drops those rows, and a dropped row has no rating. Ox Bow: 145
finishers, 2 rated. It also removed a selection bias: the survivors of a broken
division are the rows whose times *happened* to stay plausible at the wrong
distance, so their median under-states the error even where the division did
clear the gate.

Measured on the first successful run: **628,735 rows recovered (1.9%), across
35,774 divisions**; divisions clearing `MIN_FIELD` went 543,260 → 554,058.

`--gap-from ratings` restores the old gate for diffing.

The pool mean is defined in the script rather than read from the engine
**because it does not affect any decision** — every gate uses
`own_med / rating`, so a constant shared by a row and that athlete's median
cancels. It is set so each pool's median is 100, for readability and so an
athlete whose career crosses pools stays comparable (`targetFor` anchors
normalized time per pool).

### 2.3 The half that is NOT closed

1.9% is a quarter of the 2.7M finishers `find_dropped_divisions` counts in
divisions rating under 1% of their field. The premise was only half right:
`normalized_time` survives the **rating stage's** pace guard, but **nothing
dropped at or before the backfill ever gets one**. `_RESULT_DROP_XC` alone is
569,352 rows, `_DISTANCE_DROP` kills whole divisions, and a division with no
usable distance cannot be normalized at all.

Run `--coverage` to size that. It splits the still-invisible rows three ways:
no `normalized_time`, no athlete identity, or an athlete with fewer than
`--min-own-races` rated races elsewhere.

**Untested hypothesis worth pursuing:** a hand-drop for "impossible time" is
exactly what a wrong distance produces. Re-deriving those rows' normalized
time at the label distance would put them back in play. Different mechanism
from the current one; get the census first.

### 2.4 Known-wrong output to check on the next run

**Farragut State Park.** 17 divisions, all labelled 2993, snapping five
different ways (5000 ×4, 4828 ×1, 4715 ×6, 4506 ×2, 4425 ×4). All 15 in
`dist_override` carry the same meet name — *Farragut Annual Hill Climb* — so
they are one race. `snapToCourse` excludes a division's own label but not its
**siblings'**, so at a venue mislabelled several ways the wrong labels
corroborate each other.

**The ladder is a mile ruler.** 2011/2092/2172/2253/2414/2574/2655/2735/2816
are 1.25 → 1.75 miles in 0.05-mile steps. Between 2000 m and 5000 m there are
~24 rungs about 3% apart while a field's implied distance carries ±5% noise, so
the snap lands almost at random among neighbours. `snapToCorpus`'s popularity
weighting cannot overcome it: at `SNAP_SIGMA = 0.045`, 5000 at 6% error scores
2.99 against 4715 at 1% scoring 5.16.

`scripts/check_override_coherence.py` finds this class without a rebuild — it
asks whether a venue's overrides agree with **each other**, and ranks by
whether the minority answer sits on a distance the corpus barely races.

## 3. Thread: TF rated lower than XC

### 3.1 What is measured

`scripts/measure_sport_gap.py`. Puts an XC season **between two TF seasons**
and interpolates the athlete's own level across it by date, so any monotone
improvement cancels. Both directions run: a TF-XC-TF sandwich returns `C + D`
and XC-TF-XC returns `C − D`, so the mean is `D` with curvature cancelled
exactly and half the difference **is** the curvature.

`--self-test` proves the arithmetic with no database: a planted +0.02401 gap
error under 5%/yr growth comes back +0.02399, while the naive within-year
XC→TF contrast returns +0.00065 — the growth almost exactly cancels the error.

### 3.2 Results

```
D(rating) = +0.01344      both halves together
D(norm)   = -0.03878      the normalisation half   (--from norm)
D(cell)   = +0.05222      the difficulty half      (subtraction)
```

Per pool, the decomposition is the finding:

| | D(rating) | D(norm) | **D(cell)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| hs_m | +0.0223 | −0.0293 | +0.0516 |
| hs_f | +0.0031 | −0.0488 | +0.0519 |
| college_m | +0.0070 | −0.0508 | +0.0579 |
| college_f | −0.0042 | −0.0565 | +0.0524 |
| ms_m | +0.0244 | −0.0282 | +0.0526 |
| ms_f | +0.0146 | −0.0374 | +0.0519 |
| elem_m | +0.0448 | −0.0112 | +0.0560 |
| elem_f | +0.0286 | −0.0275 | +0.0560 |
| **sd** | 0.0157 | 0.0151 | **0.0024** |

**The cell half is a constant.** That is exactly what one global `bbar`
deposited in every cell should look like — the difficulties are behaving.
**All the per-pool spread lives in `normalized_time`**, not in the sport gap.

Curvature came out ≈ +0.0026 for high school and middle school and ≈ **zero**
for college. Nothing in the script knows about age; that fell out, and it is
the best evidence the method works.

### 3.3 Three hypotheses killed — do not re-litigate

1. **Growth confound.** The whole reason the tool was built. Dead: the
   estimator removes growth and `bbar` does not, yet `bbar = −0.03924` against
   `D(norm) = −0.03878`. 1.2% apart. A year of development is not in `bbar`.
2. **Distance span.** `D/span` scatters 100% of its own mean across pools.
3. **`pool_mean` carrying a sport level.** `poolPerAthlete`
   (`pair_ratings.py:57`) does `str(raw).split("|", 1)[0]` — it **strips the
   sport**. One mean per bare pool, shared, cancels in a sandwich.
   ⚠ The ★ paragraph in `conversions.pool_mean` asserts the opposite. It is
   **stale and misleading** and has not been fixed.

### 3.4 The open question

Raw discrepancy −0.0388, applied cell gap +0.0538, so the correction
**overshoots by +0.0134** — and `bbar` only contributes 0.0392 of that gap.
**Something adds ~+0.015 to the XC−TF delta before the recentre runs.**

First suspect: `shrinkByLinkage` assigns unidentified cells a per-sport
default. `linkage_check.py:413` prints both numbers every run. This costs
nothing:

```powershell
Select-String -Path logs\*\08_golive.log -Pattern "sport defaults|sport recentre|TF - XC difficulty gap"
```

If `TF − XC` there is materially wider than `bbar`, that is the whole of it,
and the fix is in how unidentified cells get defaulted — **not** in `bbar`,
which measures correctly.

If it ever does come to changing the applied gap: `recenterSport` would *take*
a `bbar` rather than compute one. The reparameterisation arithmetic is
unchanged. `Δbbar = +D` — **not** `2D`; `D` is the error in the *gap*, which
each sport carries half of.

## 4. Thread: conversions

The TF conversion table is the normalized time times `(1 + tf_default)`.
Verified: `899.93 × 1/(1 − 0.031549) = 929.25` against a printed normalized 5K
of `929.24`.

Strip the difficulty and a 132.1 rating reads 800 **1:57.4**, mile **4:24.0**,
3200 **9:29.0**, 5000 15:29.2 — a coherent athlete. So **"too fast at short
distances" was the difficulty, not the exponent**, and the within-table
exponents are fine from 800 to 3200.

**10,000 m is a real curve fault.** It sits past the last knot, so
`_evalDistancePotential` extends the boundary slope (1.099) — a deliberate
policy, but high school track has essentially no 10,000 m to constrain it. The
real 5k→10k exponent is ~1.06, which gives **31:16** against the table's
32:07.85.

Current values: `default_difficulty('XC') = +0.01746`,
`default_difficulty('TF') = −0.031549`. Note that is a **result-weighted
median**, while the recentre controls an **unweighted cell mean** and
`[link] sport defaults` prints a **linkage-weighted mean**. Three statistics,
three places. Comparing across them is not sound — that mistake was made once
in this session.

## 7. Mistakes made this session, so they are not repeated

Six of these were mine and the pattern is worth naming.

1. **A lateral before a `GROUP BY` runs per row, not per group.** Cost twice:
   `_NAME_LATERAL` and then `_LABEL_LATERAL`, each evaluating 31.6M times to
   answer 543k questions. Aggregate first, then join the lateral to the
   aggregate.
2. **`mod()` on a temp table has no statistics.** Postgres guesses, and on that
   guess a hash join over 56M rows looks cheaper than 31k index lookups. Draw
   the sample into its own analyzed table first, or use `TABLESAMPLE`.
3. **An ordered-set aggregate (`mode()`, `percentile_cont`) forces
   GroupAggregate** — a full sort, no parallel plan. Use it only where
   robustness is genuinely required.
4. **A per-item cache miss that costs a round trip is not a cache.** The
   `courseDistances` lookup was fine at 7,000 divisions and fatal at 554,000.
   Bulk-load small tables.
5. **A loop that prints nothing makes slow and hung identical.** That is why
   (4) survived two rounds of diagnosis.
6. **Comments go stale and then actively mislead.** `conversions.pool_mean`'s ★
   sent an hour down the wrong path. When a measurement contradicts a header,
   fix the header in the same commit.

And: three of my hypotheses about the sport gap were killed by measurement.
Build the test that can kill the hypothesis before arguing for the hypothesis.
